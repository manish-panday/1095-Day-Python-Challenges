Concept Explanation

Imagine a customer buys:

Laptop Bag → Rs. 2,000 × 2
Mouse      → Rs. 1,000 × 1
Keyboard   → Rs. 3,000 × 1

You need to calculate:

Item Price × Quantity
          ↓
      Subtotal
          ↓
       Discount
          ↓
      Tax
          ↓
    Final Amount

This is the basic logic behind many real-world billing systems.

Warm-up Example

Suppose:

Product price = 500
Quantity = 3

Then:

500 × 3 = 1500

In Python:

total = price * quantity

Today's Challenge
Mini Shopping Bill Generator

Create a Python program for a small shop.

The program should ask the customer for information about three products.

For each product, ask:

Product name
Product price
Quantity

Then calculate the customer's complete bill.

Requirements
Step 1 — Customer Information

Ask:

Enter customer name:
Step 2 — Product 1

Ask:

Enter product 1 name:
Enter product 1 price:
Enter product 1 quantity:

Calculate:

product 1 total = price × quantity
Step 3 — Product 2

Ask the same information.

Step 4 — Product 3

Ask the same information.

Step 5 — Calculate Subtotal

Add the three product totals.

For example:

Product 1 = 1000
Product 2 = 2000
Product 3 = 1500

Subtotal = 4500
Step 6 — Apply Discount

Use these rules:

Subtotal	Discount
Rs. 10,000 or more	15%
Rs. 5,000–9,999	10%
Rs. 2,000–4,999	5%
Below Rs. 2,000	0%

Calculate the actual discount amount.

For example:

Subtotal = 6000
Discount = 10%

Discount amount = 600
Step 7 — Calculate Amount After Discount

Formula:

Subtotal - Discount
Step 8 — Calculate Tax

Apply 13% VAT to the amount after discount.

Formula:

tax = discounted_amount × 13 / 100
Step 9 — Calculate Final Amount

Formula:

final_amount = discounted_amount + tax

Common Mistakes

Watch out for:

Multiplying the wrong price and quantity.
Adding the wrong product totals.
Applying the discount to only one product.
Calculating VAT before discount.
Using the wrong discount percentage.
Incorrect boundary conditions.
Accidentally using the original subtotal for VAT.
Repeating the entire receipt unnecessarily.
Forgetting that 13% means 13 / 100.
